# Industry Brief: Professional Services

## (Accountants, lawyers, real estate agents, financial advisors, insurance, consultants)

## The Customer's First Question
"Are they qualified and can I trust them with my problem?"

## What They Actually Need From a Website
1. Credentials + specialization (what kind of law? what kind of accounting?)
2. Evidence of experience (years, types of clients, specific outcomes)
3. How to start a conversation (contact, not commitment)
4. That they're a real person, not a corporate firm

## Psychological Job
Authority + approachability. These are high-stakes decisions. The site must project competence
without being intimidating. People want to feel like they can call without it being a big deal.

## Design Direction
**MINIMAL_MODERN** almost always. Clean, confident, organized. Signals that your affairs will be
handled the same way.

## Hero Formula
- Headline: Specialty + location. "Small business accounting for Wood River Valley." / "Real estate in Sun Valley — bought and sold right." / "Idaho estate planning, done simply."
- Subhead: The "for who" and the differentiator. "For entrepreneurs, families, and everyone in between. No corporate runaround."
- CTA: "Schedule a Free Consultation" — free removes the barrier
- Visual: Professional headshot (not stock), clean office, or abstract minimal graphic

## Must-Have Sections
1. **Hero** — specialty + free consult CTA
2. **What I Do** — specific services, plain English, no jargon
3. **Who I Work With** — types of clients, makes people self-select
4. **About** — credentials + personal story, why they do this work
5. **Process** — "How it works": Step 1, Step 2, Step 3. Demystifies engagement.
6. **Contact** — phone + email + scheduling link

## Trust Signals That Work
- Specific credentials (CPA, JD, CFP, licensed in Idaho)
- Years in practice
- Types of clients served ("I work with small businesses, landlords, and retirees")
- Named associations or bar membership
- Specific outcomes ("helped 40+ families settle estates")

## Copy Patterns That Convert
- "No billable surprise — flat fees for most services" — pricing transparency
- "Most clients reach out feeling overwhelmed. They leave with a plan." — empathy + outcome
- "I answer my own phone." — small practice signal that's a feature not a bug
- "Hailey-based, serving all of Idaho" — local + reach
- "Free 20-minute consultation — no obligation" — reduces friction to starting

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Dense paragraphs of legal/financial jargon
- No face anywhere on the site — trust requires a human
- Generic "we provide comprehensive services" — empty words
- No pricing signal at all — even "starting at" or "flat fee" helps
- Contact form only, no phone — too much friction for high-stakes decisions

## Wood River Valley Notes
- Strong second-home owner market = estate planning, property tax, real estate needs
- Many transplants from CA, NY, TX need new local accountants/lawyers
- Small business community is active and networked — one referral = many clients
- Seasonal influx creates demand spikes (real estate spring/fall, tax season)
- Personal relationships matter enormously — small town = people buy from people they know
