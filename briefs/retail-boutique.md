# Industry Brief: Retail & Boutiques

## The Customer's First Question
"What do they sell and is it worth stopping in?"

## What They Actually Need From a Website
1. A sense of what the store is / what they carry
2. Location + hours (the #1 use case for retail sites)
3. Enough personality to want to visit

## Psychological Job
Curiosity + convenience. You're not closing a sale on the site (usually) — you're convincing someone
to walk in. The site is a window display.

## Design Direction
Depends entirely on positioning:
- Premium/curated boutique → **MINIMAL_MODERN** or **WARM_EDITORIAL**
- Outdoor/gear/adventure → **MOUNTAIN_CRAFT**
- Gift shop/general → **WARM_EDITORIAL**
Never **BOLD_LOCAL** for retail unless it's a hardware or farm supply store.

## Hero Formula
- Headline: What they sell + who it's for. "Curated outdoor gear for people who actually use it." / "Sun Valley's best gift shop, locals included." / "Handmade jewelry, made in Idaho."
- Subhead: Location signal. "Find us on Main Street in Hailey."
- CTA: "See What's In" (links to products/categories) or just "Visit Us"
- Visual: Lifestyle photo of actual products or store interior — real, not staged stock

## Must-Have Sections
1. **Hero** — what you sell + invitation to visit
2. **What We Carry** — category overview or featured products, even if no full e-commerce
3. **Hours + Location** — with map, this is often WHY people come to the site
4. **About / Story** — 3-4 sentences, why this store exists, who runs it
5. **Contact** — phone + email, maybe Instagram link if active

## Trust Signals That Work
- "Locally owned" — huge in this market
- Years in business
- "Curated by [owner name]" — personal selection signal
- Active Instagram feed or photos that look current
- Local brand partnerships or "Idaho-made" angle

## Copy Patterns That Convert
- "Stop in and say hi" — casual, local, non-pushy
- "New arrivals every week" — gives reason to return
- "We ship anywhere in Idaho" — expands reach slightly without full e-commerce
- "Gift wrapping available" — practical detail that drives decisions
- "Can't find it? Ask us." — service signal

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- No hours on the site (or outdated hours) — the most common failure for retail
- Beautiful site with no indication of what they actually sell
- Stock photos of generic shopping bags / boutique interiors
- No location/map — people are deciding whether to drive there
- Instagram-only presence with no actual website

## Wood River Valley Notes
- Tourist traffic is significant — visitors actively look for "boutiques in Sun Valley/Hailey"
- Gift-buying mentality is strong — vacation = souvenir/gift budget unlocked
- "Idaho made" and "local" are genuine differentiators against Amazon
- Seasonal businesses need to clearly state if they're year-round or seasonal
- Many residents split time between Sun Valley and other cities — online presence matters even for walk-in retail
