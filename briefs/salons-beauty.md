# Industry Brief: Salons, Spas & Beauty

## The Customer's First Question
"Is this place my vibe and can I get an appointment?"

## What They Actually Need From a Website
1. Aesthetic match — does this salon feel like me?
2. Services + pricing (at least ranges)
3. How to book
4. Who's doing the work (stylists/estheticians)

## Psychological Job
Aspiration + trust. The customer is choosing someone to touch their hair/face/body. The site must
feel aspirational (I want to look like your work) and safe (these people know what they're doing).

## Design Direction
**WARM_EDITORIAL** or **MINIMAL_MODERN** depending on their positioning. Budget/family salon = warm
and approachable. Premium/luxury salon = minimal and editorial. Never BOLD_LOCAL for beauty.

## Hero Formula
- Headline: The result or the feeling. "You should love your hair." / "Sun Valley's go-to color studio." / "Book it. Own it."
- Subhead: Service focus + booking CTA hint. "Color, cuts, and treatments — now booking online."
- CTA: "Book Now" (links to booking system) or "Call to Book"
- Visual: Real work photos — hair, nails, before/afters. Genuine > polished stock.

## Must-Have Sections
1. **Hero** — vibe + book CTA
2. **Services** — with prices or "starting at $X" (people abandon if no pricing signal)
3. **Our Work / Gallery** — real photos, 6-12 minimum
4. **Meet the Team** — names, photos, specialties. People book the person, not the salon.
5. **Booking** — embedded booking link (Vagaro, StyleSeat, Square, etc.) or phone
6. **Location + Hours**

## Trust Signals That Work
- Real work photos (before/afters are gold)
- Years in business / "Serving Hailey since..."
- Named stylists with credentials
- Review quotes from Google/Yelp
- "New clients welcome" — reduces anxiety for first-timers

## Copy Patterns That Convert
- "Book online in 60 seconds" — removes friction
- "New to town? We'd love to meet you." — welcoming to transplants + seasonal residents
- "Ask for [Stylist Name] — she specializes in..." — personalizes the experience
- "Same-week appointments available" — availability signal
- Pricing as "Starting at $X" — transparency without locking in

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- No prices anywhere — drives people away
- Stock photos of hair models instead of real client work
- No individual stylist profiles — people want to know who they're booking
- Buried booking button — it must be in the hero AND sticky somewhere
- Generic "we make you beautiful" — every salon says this

## Wood River Valley Notes
- Strong seasonal influx — out-of-towners actively searching for "salon near Sun Valley"
- Resort clientele will pay premium prices if the site signals premium quality
- "Walk-ins welcome" matters for tourist traffic
- Bridal/event market is real (Sun Valley weddings are a thing)
- Locals are loyal — emphasize community connection for retention
