# Industry Brief: Wellness, Fitness & Health

## (Yoga studios, gyms, personal trainers, massage, chiropractic, acupuncture, therapists)

## The Customer's First Question
"Will this actually help me and is it for someone like me?"

## What They Actually Need From a Website
1. What they offer and what outcome it provides
2. Who the practitioner is — credentials + personality
3. How to book a session or class
4. Enough trust to make a first appointment

## Psychological Job
Safety + transformation. People are putting their bodies or mental health in someone's hands.
The site must feel calm, credible, and human. Show the person, not just the service.

## Design Direction
**WARM_EDITORIAL** for yoga, massage, therapy, holistic.
**MINIMAL_MODERN** for performance fitness, personal training, sports rehab.
**MOUNTAIN_CRAFT** for outdoor fitness, adventure wellness, hiking guides.

## Hero Formula
- Headline: The outcome, not the service. "Move better. Feel stronger." / "Stress exists. So does relief." / "Your body is capable of more."
- Subhead: What + first-visit signal. "Massage therapy in Hailey — accepting new clients."
- CTA: "Book a Session" or "Schedule a Free Consult" (free intro reduces barrier)
- Visual: Real space or real practitioner — serene, clean, genuine. Not stock yoga poses.

## Must-Have Sections
1. **Hero** — outcome + book CTA
2. **Services** — clear descriptions, what to expect, duration, price
3. **Meet [Name]** — credentials, background, why they do this work. Personality matters.
4. **How It Works** — first visit process, what to bring, what to expect. Reduces new-client anxiety.
5. **Booking** — integrated scheduler (Mindbody, Acuity, Calendly, etc.) or phone
6. **Location + Hours**

## Trust Signals That Work
- Credentials prominently placed (LMT, RYT-500, NASM-CPT, etc.)
- Years in practice
- "Accepting new clients" — active signal, not just static page
- Before/after or testimonial quotes
- Specific specialties (prenatal massage, sports injury, anxiety + depression)

## Copy Patterns That Convert
- "First session $X" — intro pricing removes risk
- "I work with people who..." — specific audience creates belonging
- "Not sure if this is right for you? Let's talk." — low-pressure entry point
- "Hailey's only [specialty]" — scarcity + local authority
- Outcome language: "leave feeling..." "most clients notice..."

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Bio that's all credentials and no personality
- "We offer holistic wellness solutions" — meaningless
- No pricing (especially for massage and fitness — people won't call just to ask)
- Generic stock photos of candles or people meditating
- Overwhelming service menu with no guidance on where to start

## Wood River Valley Notes
- Extremely strong market — the WRV demographic over-indexes on wellness spending
- Seasonal residents often have home practitioners but look for local options while in Sun Valley
- Outdoor athletes are a major segment — sports recovery, PT, functional fitness
- "Year-round" vs "seasonal" matters — state it clearly
- Many practitioners are solo/independent — personal brand is the whole business
