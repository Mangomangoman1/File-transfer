# Industry Brief: Contractors & Trades
## (Plumbers, Electricians, HVAC, Roofers, Handymen, Builders)

## The Customer's First Question
"Are they legit and can they come soon?"

## What They Actually Need From a Website
1. Proof they're real and reliable (license, insurance, years in business)
2. What they do + what areas they serve
3. How to contact them right now
4. Evidence others have used them (reviews, photos)

## Psychological Job
Eliminate doubt fast. People calling a contractor are often stressed — something broke or they have
a project that's overdue. They want to feel safe choosing you, not excited about you.

## Design Direction
**BOLD_LOCAL** first choice. Strong, confident, zero fluff. Or **MOUNTAIN_CRAFT** if they lean into
the Idaho/outdoors identity (common for custom builders and remodelers up here).

## Hero Formula
- Headline: Credibility + service area. "Licensed Contractor Serving the Wood River Valley Since 2009." / "Same-Day Plumbing Repair in Hailey & Ketchum."
- Subhead: Scope + differentiator. "Residential and commercial. No job too small."
- CTA: "Call Now — (208) XXX-XXXX" as a tappable button, nothing else matters more
- Visual: Real job photos (before/after, finished work), NOT stock photos of tools

## Must-Have Sections
1. **Hero** — credibility statement + call now button
2. **Services** — clean list of what they do, no long paragraphs
3. **Why Us** — 3-4 trust signals as icons/cards (licensed, insured, X years, local)
4. **Work Photos** — real photos, even phone quality beats stock
5. **Reviews** — Google review quotes, at least 2-3
6. **Service Area** — list towns explicitly ("Hailey, Ketchum, Sun Valley, Bellevue, Carey")
7. **Contact / Call** — phone huge, form optional

## Trust Signals That Work
- License number displayed ("License #RCE-XXXXX")
- Insurance mentioned ("Fully insured")
- Years in business ("Serving the Valley since 2008")
- Number of jobs completed ("500+ projects completed")
- Response time promise ("We answer the phone. No voicemail runaround.")

## Copy Patterns That Convert
- "We show up when we say we will" — addresses the #1 contractor complaint
- "Free estimates" — reduces friction
- "Licensed and insured in Idaho" — specificity builds trust
- "Family owned" — strong signal in a small town
- "We've worked on half the homes in Ketchum" — local social proof

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- No phone number above fold (criminal for trades)
- Generic "quality work at fair prices" — says nothing
- Only a contact form with no phone — contractors need to be callable
- Stock photos of smiling workers — screams template
- No service area listed — people wonder if you'll come to their neighborhood

## Wood River Valley Notes
- Second-home market is huge — wealthy out-of-towners need trusted local contractors badly
- Seasonal demand spikes: spring (winter damage), fall (winterizing)
- Property management companies are B2B goldmine — one relationship = many jobs
- "Sun Valley certified" or "resort community experience" is a signal that resonates with high-end clients
- Response time matters more here — nobody has time to chase contractors on vacation
