# Industry Brief: Restaurants & Cafes

## The Customer's First Question
"Is the food good and is it worth the drive/walk?"

## What They Actually Need From a Website
1. Sense of the atmosphere — is this my kind of place?
2. Menu (or at least what kind of food)
3. Hours + location
4. Whether they need a reservation

## Psychological Job
Make them hungry and make them feel like they'd fit in there.

## Design Direction
**WARM_EDITORIAL** almost always. Restaurants are about feeling. Warmth, texture, appetite.
Use food photography aggressively if available. If not, use bold color and typography to convey atmosphere.

## Hero Formula
- Headline: The feeling, not the food. "Sun Valley's favorite after-ski spot." / "Scratch cooking, every morning." / "Where locals actually eat."
- Subhead: What + hours hint. "Breakfast & lunch, Tuesday through Sunday."
- CTA: "See the Menu" (primary) + "Call for Reservations" (secondary)
- Visual: Best food photo available, warm overlay, or strong color field

## Must-Have Sections
1. **Hero** — atmosphere + CTA
2. **Menu** — even a PDF link or "what we're known for" highlights
3. **Hours + Location** — with embedded map or address
4. **About / Story** — 3-4 sentences, personal, who's cooking
5. **Reservations / Contact** — phone prominent

## Trust Signals That Work
- Google review count + star rating
- "Open since [year]" / "Family owned since [year]"
- Quote from a real review in big text
- Press mentions if any ("As seen in Sun Valley Magazine")

## Copy Patterns That Convert
- "Made from scratch daily" — specificity beats generics
- "Order ahead for pickup" — adds convenience signal
- "Locals welcome, visitors too" — playful community signal
- Use the owner's first name somewhere — "Chef Maria" not "our executive chef"

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Menu as a PDF that requires download
- No photos of actual food — vibes only doesn't work here
- Generic "We serve quality food in a warm atmosphere" — says nothing
- Hours buried in the footer only
- No phone number above fold

## Wood River Valley Notes
- Sun Valley attracts wealthy out-of-towners who will pay for quality
- Locals are price-sensitive and loyalty-driven
- Après-ski culture is real — dinner reservations + after-hours angle matters
- Many restaurants are seasonal — note if relevant
- Outdoor seating is a selling point 9 months of the year
